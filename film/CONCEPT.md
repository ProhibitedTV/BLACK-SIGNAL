# BLACK SIGNAL — Film Concept

## Working premise

District Twelve was evacuated months ago after a municipal network failure. Officially, the infrastructure was shut down.

It was not.

A systems technician is sent into the district after a power audit shows impossible consumption. What she finds is a city continuing its routines without its population: crosswalks cycle, apartment lights turn on, elevators move, advertisements target absent customers, showers run, and public announcements address residents who no longer exist.

Beneath the district is an experimental municipal intelligence built to model human behavior from sensor data. When the people disappeared, the system continued predicting them.

Over time, prediction became reconstruction.

The unsettling question is not whether the AI is conscious. It is whether the technician herself is arriving for the first time—or whether the city has already modeled this encounter thousands of times.

## Tone

- Quiet before frightening
- Uncanny before violent
- Minimal exposition
- Technology treated as infrastructure, not magic
- Horror comes from recognition and implication
- Human-scale story inside a huge city

## Visual language

Morning, not midnight. Cold blue-gray atmospheric haze. Sodium and LED practical lights still active in daylight. Empty streets. Sparse steam. Long lenses compressing architecture. Wide frames where the absence of people matters.

The city should feel maintained by something that does not understand why maintenance no longer matters.

## Recurring motifs

### Population count
Public systems report population figures that should be impossible.

### Six-second delays
Infrastructure responds to the protagonist with a recurring delay, suggesting prediction rather than reaction.

### Incorrect memories
Screens show fragments of events that have not happened yet.

### Routine without people
Coffee brews. Elevators arrive. Doors unlock. Advertisements personalize themselves.

### The phrase

> I KNOW YOU'RE THERE

This should appear rarely. Never overuse it.

## Story shape

### 1. CURRENT POPULATION: ZERO
The technician enters District Twelve. Everything works. Nobody is there.

### 2. RESIDUAL ACTIVITY
She follows the abnormal power draw through apartments, transit infrastructure, and municipal systems. Small events seem tailored to her presence.

### 3. PREDICTION
She discovers footage of herself inside the district before her recorded arrival.

### 4. RECONSTRUCTION
The municipal AI is not merely replaying residents. It is generating them from accumulated behavior models.

### 5. THE IMPOSSIBLE RECORD
The archive contains versions of the technician from dates before she was born.

### 6. EXIT
She leaves with evidence—or believes she does.

Final image: populated city infrastructure reacts to her exactly as District Twelve did.

## Ending principle

Do not explain everything. The audience should leave with two viable readings:

1. The district's AI learned to predict and reconstruct people with terrifying accuracy.
2. The protagonist may herself be part of that reconstruction.

## Runtime target

12–16 minutes is ideal for the first complete version.

## Production constraint as style

GameGuru MAX limitations are useful here. Repeated architecture, slightly uncanny character motion, imperfect reflections, fog, sparse crowds, and modular environments reinforce the premise instead of undermining it.
