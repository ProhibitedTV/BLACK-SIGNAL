## Camera principles

- Favor static compositions and slow deliberate moves over game-like free-camera motion.
- Let empty frames breathe.
- Use long lenses for architecture whenever possible to make the district feel compressed and enormous.
- Avoid excessive Dutch angles or glitch effects; the world should feel physically real.
- Save handheld/shaky movement for later escalation, if used at all.

## Environment requirements

For this sequence we need only:

1. One hero boulevard with deep sightline.
2. One checkpoint / gate.
3. One commercial walkway with multiple storefronts.
4. One kiosk or vending area.
5. One visible apartment facade with controllable window lighting.
6. Elevated rail or convincing distant transit element.
7. Large digital billboard / display surface.

These can overlap spatially. The boulevard, checkpoint, walkway, and apartment block do not need to be part of a fully navigable city.

## Sound requirements

- Low electrical infrastructure hum
- Far transit clunks / rail movement
- Sparse HVAC
- Crosswalk chirp or signal tone
- Street sweeper motor
- Coffee machine mechanism
- Radio voice
- Distant ringing telephones
- No score until at least the timestamp reveal, unless an extremely minimal tonal bed is used

## First production test

Before building the entire sequence, produce shots **2, 5, 7, 17, and 26** as visual proof-of-concept frames. If those five images sell the world, proceed with the full opening.
